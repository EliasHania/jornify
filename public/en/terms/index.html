  • Your company must inform its employees about the working time record and the use of location when clocking in.
  • Jornify is in a trial phase and is free while that phase lasts.

2. The service

Jornify allows the company to keep the daily working time record of its employees (art. 34.9 of the Spanish Workers' Statute): clock-ins, clock-outs and breaks with server time, on site (with workplace verification) or remotely (without location), one or more workplaces, each employee's working terms and schedule, public holidays and absences, corrections with the employee's agreement or disagreement, a monthly summary for payroll, pseudonymised access for the legal employee representatives, temporary access for the Labour Inspectorate and reports in PDF, Excel and CSV. It also includes clocking in from the web dashboard and from a kiosk (tablet) with PIN or QR, requests and approval of holidays and leave with their balance, shift rota, hour bank, monthly sign-off of the summary by each employee, and notifications in the app and on the dashboard with push notifications. The app and the dashboard are available in Spanish, English and French.

During the trial phase the service is offered free of charge and as is, with no guarantee of availability. We'll notify you in advance before charging or changing these terms. The company must download and keep its reports regularly: it is the party required to keep the record for 4 years.

3. The company's obligations

  • Inform each employee, before they start using the app, about the processing of their data (art. 13 GDPR) and, expressly, clearly and unambiguously, about the use of location when clocking in (art. 90 LOPDGDD). The app shows a notice, but it does not replace the information the company must provide.
  • Organise the working time record in accordance with the collective agreement, a company agreement or, failing that, after consulting the legal employee representatives (art. 34.9 of the Workers' Statute).
  • Offer another way of clocking in to anyone who cannot or does not want to use their personal phone.
  • Make the record available to employees, their representatives and the Labour Inspectorate when requested, and keep it for 4 years.
  • Assess whether it needs a data protection impact assessment for its specific case.
  • Record in Jornify only the type and dates of absences, never diagnoses or medical reasons.
  • Mark as legal employee representative only those who actually are, and share Labour Inspectorate access links only with the Inspectorate.
  • Not use the app to monitor employees' location outside the moment of clocking in or out.
  • If it uses the kiosk, place the tablet at the workplace, not share its activation link and deactivate it if it's lost. Printed badges are personal: the company must cancel any that are lost.

4. Data processing agreement (art. 28.3 GDPR)

Subject matter, duration and purpose

Jornify processes personal data on behalf of the company for the sole purpose of providing the working time record service described above, while the company uses the service and, afterwards, during the legal retention periods set out in section 4.6.

Data and data subjects

  • Data subjects: the company's employees and administrators.
  • Data: identification data (name, surname, ID number, email), employment data (company, role, status, type of working time, hours, part-time percentage, schedule, remote work authorisation, type of contract, representative status), clock entries (type, date and time, work mode and workplace), location at the moment of clocking in on site and its accuracy, absences and holiday or leave requests (type, dates, hours, comments and answer), holiday balance, shifts, hour bank, monthly summary sign-offs (agreement, comment, date, device and fingerprint), corrections with their reasons and answers, kiosk credentials (only encrypted fingerprints of the PIN and QR), notifications and push notification identifiers, preferred language, and the log of Labour Inspectorate accesses (date, IP and browser).
  • No biometric data is processed. The only possible special category is the fact that an absence is due to sick leave (without diagnosis), which the company processes to fulfil its employment obligations (art. 9.2.b GDPR).

Jornify's obligations as processor

  • Process the data only on the company's documented instructions, which are those in this agreement and those the company gives when using the app.
  • Ensure the confidentiality of anyone who accesses the data.
  • Apply appropriate security measures (art. 32 GDPR): encrypted connections, access control by user and company in the database, clock entries that cannot be modified or deleted (corrections are stored as new records with their author, the employee's agreement or disagreement and the original entry) and chained with a cryptographic fingerprint.
  • Help the company respond to employees' rights (access, rectification, erasure, etc.) and comply with its obligations regarding security, breach notification and impact assessments.
  • Notify the company, without undue delay, of any security breach affecting its data.
  • Make available to the company the information needed to demonstrate compliance and allow reasonable audits.

Sub-processors

The company authorises Jornify to use these sub-processors: Supabase (database, authentication and infrastructure, with data hosted in the European Union, Ireland), Google (maps and Google sign-in) and Expo, 650 Industries, Inc. (sending push notifications, only with the phone identifier and the text of the notification). Jornify will give advance notice of any change of sub-processor so the company can object. Where a sub-processor may access data from outside the European Economic Area, this will be done with GDPR safeguards (standard contractual clauses or the EU–US Data Privacy Framework).

4.6. End of the service and retention

If the company stops using the service or dissolves its company in the app, its employees can no longer clock in. Working time records are kept blocked for 4 years from the last clock entry, because the law requires them to be kept, and are then deleted automatically. The company can request a full copy of its records beforehand.
